Remote work has changed how many people work. What used to be an occasional perk is now part of the routine for millions of professionals. A 2023 Gallup survey found that 52% of U.S. employees who can work remotely have hybrid arrangements, showing how much workers value flexibility.

The shift also has practical benefits for companies. Remote workers tend to be more productive and satisfied, while employers can reduce office costs. Studies have found that remote employees save about 55 minutes a day by skipping the commute, leaving more time for work or personal life.

There are drawbacks, too. Teams can lose their sense of connection, and remote employees may feel isolated. Managers need clear ways to keep distributed teams coordinated and engaged.

Good remote work depends on communication, trust, and reasonable boundaries. Those habits matter whether someone has worked remotely for years or is just getting started.
