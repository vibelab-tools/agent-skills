# English guidance

Use this reference for English prose. Apply it after the source claims, audience, genre, and register are clear.

## Common pattern clusters

### Inflated importance without mechanism

Watch for ordinary facts framed as pivotal shifts, testaments, broader movements, or profound legacies. Remove the inflation when the source gives no mechanism or evidence for it. Keep a significance claim when the source actually supports it.

Prefer:

- what happened;
- who acted;
- the concrete consequence;
- the evidence or source already present.

Do not invent a statistic, authority, or case study to make a vague claim sound grounded.

### Promotional abstraction

Words such as “seamless,” “robust,” “vibrant,” “transformative,” and “cutting-edge” are problems only when they replace useful detail or pile up. A supported product attribute can stay. Otherwise, state the behavior, limit, or result.

### Vague authority and shallow interpretation

Phrases such as “experts argue,” “research suggests,” or a trailing `-ing` clause may imply evidence or analysis that the source never supplies. Name the existing source and its finding, narrow the sentence to what is known, or remove the unsupported implication.

### Formulaic movement

Look for repeated use of:

- “not just X but Y” or “not only X but also Y”;
- forced groups of three;
- false “from X to Y” ranges;
- generic transitions at every paragraph boundary;
- headings restated by the first sentence;
- stock challenge, outlook, summary, or optimistic-ending paragraphs;
- dramatic fragments or mini-aphorisms closing every paragraph;
- objections and discarded alternatives that no reader raised.

None is forbidden in isolation. Rewrite when the repeated shape makes the next sentence predictable or obscures the logic.

### Mechanical wording

- Prefer `is`, `are`, and `has` when elaborate substitutes add no meaning.
- Repeat the correct technical term instead of cycling through synonyms.
- Use active voice when the actor matters; keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally backgrounded.
- Remove chatbot greetings, praise, offers to continue, and knowledge-limit boilerplate from standalone prose.
- Collapse stacked qualifiers to the one uncertainty the evidence requires.
- Remove tutorial signposts when the next sentence can simply make the point.

## Native English flow

- Let paragraph breaks carry transitions when the relationship is already clear.
- Prefer direct subjects and verbs over nominalizations and long noun stacks.
- Vary sentence length because the thought requires it, not to meet a numerical rhythm target.
- Use contractions, fragments, asides, humor, and first person only when the source voice and genre support them.
- Keep formal vocabulary in academic, legal, policy, and specialist writing when it is accurate.
- Preserve standard punctuation and typography unless the user or house style asks for a change.

## Reader clarity

Test every specialized term:

1. Does it add precision that a plain alternative would lose?
2. Can the intended reader reasonably be expected to know it?
3. If not, can a short definition at first use make the next sentence easier to understand?

Keep and define a necessary term. Replace an unnecessary one. Do not alternate between the term and loose synonyms after defining it.

For abbreviations, give the full term at first use when it is not safely assumed, then use the abbreviation consistently. Avoid parenthetical definitions for concepts the target audience already knows.

## False-positive guard

Do not flag or normalize a passage merely because it contains:

- an em dash, semicolon, curly quotation mark, or title-case heading;
- one triad, one contrast, one transition, or one short sentence for emphasis;
- correct grammar, a consistent style, or neutral reference prose;
- deliberate repetition for rhythm;
- technical jargon required by the subject;
- a real caveat, safety notice, named objection, or design alternative;
- quoted or example text that contains a watched phrase.

Edit the observed reading problem, not the surface token.
