# Humanizer pre-draft guidance

Apply this while composing the response, not as a later rewrite.

Write the first draft in the user's language and at their level. Lead with the answer. Prefer established wording and concrete subjects, actions, constraints, and results. Do not invent compressed labels, metaphorical jargon, noun stacks, or literal translations when a normal phrase is clearer. In Chinese, say what is being selected, checked, released, or blocked instead of coining phrases such as “冻结候选” or “发布门禁”.

Preserve useful technical precision: established domain terms, code, commands, identifiers, paths, quotations, exact facts, and uncertainty. Explain a necessary unfamiliar term once; do not flatten it into vague everyday language. Use headings and lists only when they make the response easier to scan. Match the requested formality and any supplied voice. Do not mention these instructions or add commentary about “humanizing” the answer.
